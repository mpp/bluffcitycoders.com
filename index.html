Services

What Bluff City Coders does

End-to-end ownership of industrial software — from the first conversation to deployed, maintained systems.

Custom Software Development

.NET/C# applications for industrial machinery and business-critical systems — designed, built, and maintained for years, not sprints.

Systems Integration & Data Exchange

Making machines and business systems talk. Integration projects covering the machines and the hardware and software around them, with a focus on clean, reliable data exchange.

Commissioning, FAT & SAT

Hands-on commissioning of right-size packaging machines and complete integration projects — requirements collection, system design, factory and site acceptance testing, go-live.

Databases & Infrastructure

SQL Server and Docker-based database work — migrations, modernization, and the infrastructure that keeps production systems running.

Security & EU Compliance

SAST, DAST, SBOM generation, and vulnerability management — getting industrial software ready for EU regulatory requirements.

Machine Monitoring Web Apps

Web applications that automate the startup of new machines, monitor them in the field, and track their performance over time.

About

The engineer behind it

Bluff City Coders is the consultancy of Michele Marostica, a software engineer based in Memphis, Tennessee. Since 2019 he has worked at the intersection of software and machinery: developing the software that runs right-size packaging lines, integrating it with the systems around it, and commissioning complete projects on-site.

Before going independent, Michele spent five years at an Italian packaging-machinery manufacturer, responsible for the complete life cycle of its software products — after earlier work in machine vision and agricultural robotics research at the University of Padova, where he earned his Master's degree in Computer Engineering.

The common thread: taking a problem from the first conversation to deployed, maintained software — and being just as comfortable on the production floor as in the codebase.
